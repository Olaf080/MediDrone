# MediDrone

About 275,000 Europeans have an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest each year, the American statistics prove even more concerning at 350,000. At best, 10% of them survive to hospital discharge. What is the bottleneck ? During cardiac arrest, the heart suddenly stops pumping blood, cutting off oxygen and nutrients to the brain and vital organs. Tissue damage begins within minutes and worsens rapidly the longer blood flow stays stopped. Furthermore, the only viable treatment a bystander can give is CPR, that is, if they do not have an AED (automated external defibrillator) at hand. 

A 2025 study by the Cardiac Arrest Registry to Enhance Survival (CARES) investigated out-of-hospital cardiac arrests : 13.9 % of patients who arrested in public had a bystander applied AED, 71.4 % of arrests happen in a residential setting (17.5 % in public and 11.1 % in nursing homes) and finally the median EMS response time was 7.6 min. The dots aren't hard to connect, reducing cardiac arrest fatality depends on cutting down the time to response and providing resources to apply AED's.
